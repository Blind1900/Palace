Explanations

Four adjacent units share one terrain world and one 60 Hz simulation clock. Their initial positions differ; compare distance from each starting point, not absolute X. Moving units automatically reverse at horizontal crop ends. Jets make curved turns and return toward their selected altitude. Default tank 3x, hopper 2x and jet 4x sizes are visual proportions only: no passengers or cockpit occupancy are implemented. Size controls reset the scene safely. Each source pixel expands to a whole-cell block with matching collision, mirroring and foot anchor. Friendly units overlap freely; opposing collision envelopes cannot pass through each other. Units still add no structural mass.

Tank: gradually reaches 36 cells/s over 1.5 seconds, with independent reversible aiming. Hopper: stationary idle holds the first default frame; ground movement follows the GIF's forward hopping rhythm, at a steady 48 cells/s with no backward loop reset; complete walking frames keep their feet anchored to the ground. Jump plays once and holds its last pose during ascent; fall/glide uses the airborne poses. Boost bursts at 192 cells/s for 0.18 seconds on dry ground or in air, without an upward impulse. It plays the complete boost clip faster, then returns to jump/fall in air. Ground bursts coast for two seconds; air bursts coast for 0.35 seconds while gravity continues. Ground boost poses keep their feet aligned and hold the final frame during coasting. Airborne frames stay intact as authored; the renderer does not splice wings from another frame or clip the body to force a shared pose. Takeoff eases the complete frame into its airborne position. Two boost charges and the separate two-second airborne glide are lab budgets. Jet: sustained flight and curved direction changes, not a ground walker. These are explicit lab defaults; deployment, boost recharge and route planning are not implemented.

Partial submersion halves horizontal speed; water supplies no buoyancy, so units sink. Full coverage of the rendered opaque body latches drowning and disables movement commands. Automated ground units wait on dry land before entering water; supported dry bridges remain usable. A jet may fly above water, but a submerged jet also sinks and drowns. Units never erase or displace particle water.

The top reference strip replays the original bullet GIF. The missile is a derived preview: each bullet pixel expands 3x horizontally and 2x vertically, preserving palette and GIF timing. This is a longer, thicker missile, not another imported GIF. The strip demonstrates the original animated pixel travel, not weapon damage. The strip and detached streaks in the jet's shooting GIF are visual previews, not extra damaging projectiles.

Choose a firing unit, then click the preview to aim/fire. Run or Step advances the shot beyond the GIF's final frame until it hits or exits the crop. Dragging pans without firing. Shots follow the clicked direction; they are not homing. Bullet, heavier shell and longer/thicker missile sizes use the reference artwork. Friendly fire is disabled. Shooting targets adds opposing units and a free lab building frame. Hits reduce unit HP; destroyed units cannot fire or move under commands. Building/terrain hits convert actual particle cells to debris; impact fragments use the engine's falling, water-displacement and support physics, not cosmetic particles. Up to four ammo-fragment cells per hit are explicit free lab material and audited separately. Flight is swept kinematic physics with water drag, not a new particle material or full blast-pressure/fire simulation. City HM/SM, core death, capture and live-game combat are not changed.
