Physical tunnel and mining lab

First stage: free excavation with no HM yield. Connect the shaft and travel tunnel to the purple resource node and a separate underground storage chamber. Mining starts immediately upon a valid connection: two three-pixel miners begin with node HM and carry it into the chamber. There is no separate HM-production building.


  
Travel checks a dry, empty 3-by-3 clearance using a shortest-route search; obstruction/flooding makes miners
    wait without discarding cargo. Each carries up to 5 HM, harvests every 30 ticks at the finite 300-HM node,
    after the initial connection harvest, and moves one cell per two ticks. The chamber holds 100 HM; space is reserved for cargo in transit.
    Withdrawals resume harvesting. Terrain, water and HM ledgers are audited independently.

  
This lab uses a predefined excavated corridor and chamber, not manual tunnel placement, structural lining,
    save integration or construction costs. Chamber outlines and stored-HM fill are logical overlays,
    not physical wall material. Miners overlap one another; flooding pauses travel rather than killing miners.
    Reset restores the original world. Breaching the basin opens actual terrain and allows physical water flow.
    The intended game construction charge belongs to tunnels themselves, not to a production building;
    this lab retains free excavation pending the tunnel-cost rule.

  
Orange one-pixel transporters collect HM from tunnel storage first, or the blue existing-core
    stockpile when it has no reachable stored HM. Dark ghost markers reserve a request, but no seed or road
    becomes physical until all required HM arrives. Each load is at most 5 HM; construction costs 1 HM per
    placed pixel (three-pixel demonstration seeds and a ten-pixel road). Dry paths are checked at every move;
    blocked cargo stays with its transporter. Core stock starts at 50 HM and does not regenerate.
    These are placement/delivery tests, not growing full buildings or full-map placement integration.
    Worker paths are logical pixel routes, without gravity, worker combat or road-speed bonuses yet.
