Full-map population skyscrapers are 15-20 times the occupied standing trooper height: exact quarter-size trooper artwork is 6.25 cells tall within 7 grid rows, so level 1-7 towers are 94-125 cells tall. The source receives empty sky rows above the entire terrain, shifting water and tunnels equally without changing their shapes. A real quarter-size ground trooper stands beside the first blue population tower; it obeys collision/gravity, but is not assigned patrol or automatic firing. Towers use paid one-pixel structural uprights and repeating physical floors connected to their independent foundations. Full-map population and military cores are spaced 6-8 cells apart, rather than 20-24; population floors are 7 cells apart. This reduces hollow volume while retaining worker passages and local storage. Farm silos keep their functional food cavities. All four mobile unit types use reduced artwork and collision masks, not CSS scaling. Fine-resolution drawing retains every original source pixel, color and GIF frame duration. Zoom in to inspect native detail; whole-map views necessarily show less detail. Launcher missiles rise and descend toward the opposing faction. Interceptors detect hostile offensive missiles up to 960 cells away and retain homing pursuit. Launchers check the whole curved projectile path and raise their aim around intervening terrain. If available sky cannot provide a clear arc, they hold fire and retry rather than shooting through obstructions. Missiles and scattered projectiles leave no ammunition residue; damaged terrain and collapsing buildings still produce normal debris. Ground-supported rubble becomes solid terrain after ten seconds at rest, keeping its pixels and joining the surface outline. Movement resets that timer; rubble on structural roofs remains debris. Roads, placement and ground units recognize the raised surface. Each faction retains its Asian, Gothic or Modern design and blue, red or neutral team accents across population, military and farm buildings. Population roofs have paid physical upturned eaves, pointed crowns or flat rooflines respectively. Lattice, pointed-arch and horizontal glazing details are visual only; they do not fill worker passages or add collision mass. Each tower gets a finite lab HM allowance equal to its frame-cell count plus 1,000 HM, rather than running out under the former small-building allowance. Structural support capacity in this enlarged full-map lab is explicitly 1,024; legacy labs retain 128. Run simulation or build the preview to construct the towers; the initial outlines are only plans.

Full-map population and military buildings require farm-delivered SM. A shortage pauses growth and unit production without damaging buildings or removing existing units. Smaller crops and the military demo can enable the same rules with the button above. These interior storage markings use empty floor space, not shared structural walls. HM repairs still use the existing paid pixel-repair carriers and finite starting core allowances; replenishment from tunnels is not yet linked to this map.

Open shared combat area: bases, units, roads, missiles and terrain impacts

Three separate buildings share one 90-cell-high terrain, water and debris crop sampled from a generated map. Crop width follows the selected sites; spacing is not forced. Each has its own 1000 HM budget, 27 buried core pixels and connecting floors. One physics tick advances the shared world, then each building advances construction without stepping physics again. No live-game economy, units or saves are changed.

Each dropped seed begins a quick, visibly animated foundation stage: middle 3-by-3 core first, buried floors branching outward and progressively excavating their basement columns, then adjacent cores and surface pillar seeds. Each building grows only one pixel every two simulation ticks; neither cores nor floor branches pop into place as a batch. The foundation takes a few seconds, and each basement column opens as its floor/extension column finishes. No HM is spent until the free foundation stage finishes and blueprint reveal permits paid construction. Water/debris block growing foundation cells; only terrain is excavated. Destroying every formed core permanently stops that building, including unfinished seed growth.

Sites must fit the same crop without flattening terrain. No suitable group stops generation explicitly. Shared water is conserved inside the closed crop. Repair pixels choose shortest physically clear routes to the nearest reachable available target and can overlap immediately, without traffic detours or yielding. Each building owns its paid deliveries and unique target reservations. Water, terrain interiors and debris still block routes. Completion stops automatic deliveries; only an explicit lab upgrade or manual repair reopens completed work. Terrain restoration is disabled during seed growth so it cannot undo or pre-empt progressive excavation.

Military defense placements are a deterministic, read-only lab layer. Orange turrets, purple guard troops, red missile launchers and cyan interceptors appear after construction. Turrets mount on left/right roof corners and point outward toward those edges. Logical mounting legs accommodate roof steps up to two cells, including silo caps. Each base also considers the nearest 2 added population/farm neighbors at levels 1-3, 3 at levels 4-6, and 4 at level 7, within a horizontal gap of 40 columns plus 4 per additional level. Incomplete or dead hosts receive no rooftop defenses; missing slots are reported. Multiple bases share unique placement slots, without writing to the host's physical structure or spending its materials. Upgrades temporarily suspend that base's placements; damaged anchors or obstructed air remove affected placements immediately. These are placement previews only, not functional combat, troop routing, interception or hacking.
