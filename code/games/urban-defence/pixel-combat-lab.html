Bounded shared combat fixture

One 1100 × 400 physical cell world: two opposing military hangars, population towers, steel-blue farm silos/amber stores/wheat, a water-filled basin, hill and mountain. Units use the city scale with comparison-relative proportions: trooper 1x, tank 3x, mech/hopper 2x, plane 4x, then all are reduced by four into physical cells. Fine sprite details, original timing and integer collision/foot anchors are preserved. Tank passengers remain infantry-sized.

Population buildings and hangars have troop-sized entrances. Infantry walks into real openings; larger tanks drive the background route behind every building (friendly or enemy), visible through tinted glass windows, but stop at an enemy core. Mechs pass building walls sideways and land on roofs and floors, walking on them like one-way platforms. Tank and mech fire passes their own building layer; enemy structure still intercepts shots, so attackers breach it, chipping through walls toward their target. Loose debris never stops a round or a unit. With no closer enemy unit, everyone advances on the enemy core, opens fire within 200 cells (no cross-map sniping), digs out rubble settled over it and keeps attacking until it is destroyed. Terrain, water and enemy units remain physical obstacles. Planes never use the background route: flying into any building crashes the plane, damages actual building cells and emits velocity-driven sparks.

Automatic transport and death effects

  
Watch the automatic dry-route test:
    starts running immediately with base spawning, troop transport and auto-fire enabled,
    rooftop weapons initially off so carriers can demonstrate deployment. No movement or firing input is needed.
    Changing the scenario resets the physical world; the original hills and water remain unchanged in the original scenario.

  

  
Tanks automatically load up to three newly issued troopers while grounded inside their living hangar.
    Passengers count toward the four-living-unit and 64-lifetime-unit limits, travel protected inside the tank,
    and do not move, fire or occupy world cells until deployed. Tanks carry them toward the opposing hangar's
    160-cell approach, or deploy at a nearby hostile frontline within 120 cells of midfield.
    Every half second they attempt to deploy one trooper directly from their rear hatch into dry, supported
    space clear of debris and other units. Troops briefly walk out away from the hatch before advancing;
    they never appear at an enemy's position or teleport to a distant clear spot.
    Blocked deployment waits and retries without deleting material or passengers. Tanks with cargo prioritize
    delivery over firing, then join combat after unloading. Near water, a friendly hopper automatically approaches,
    grips the tank with its feet, lifts it, flies across and lowers it onto checked dry ground.
    Passengers remain aboard. Automatic wet-route loading reserves a living-unit slot for a hopper
    (two passengers instead of three). Blocked flight paths or landing sites wait and report the reason;
    losing the hopper releases the tank to real gravity. Disabling loading prevents new cargo,
    but already loaded tanks finish their missions. Destroying or drowning a carrier loses its remaining passengers.

  
Actual troop deaths emit a short red pixel burst; tanks, hoppers and jets emit yellow/orange sparks.
    Hits flash the entire unit bright red for 0.12 seconds and release a small burst at the contact point.
    Death fragments originate from sampled occupied artwork, burst only a short distance, fall under strong
    260-cell gravity with air drag,
    sweep one cell at a time against terrain, structures and debris, bounce with energy loss, and slow in water.
    They fade automatically and persist after
    the dead unit is removed. The shared effect cap is 512 particles, with suppressed issuance reported.
    These are cosmetic particles, not damaging projectiles or physical material; conservation and resource
    accounting are unchanged.

  

  
Bounded milestone, not live-game integration

  
Loose debris no longer traps units. Movement scales linearly with the fraction of collision pixels touching
    debris: 100% speed when clear, 67.5% at half coverage and 35% when fully covered.
    The current and next one-cell positions are checked so entry is slowed too. Debris is neither deleted
    nor treated as solid support. Resting or already-overlapping debris causes no HP damage.
    Resting rubble stays loose in living collision cells and can harden into terrain only after they clear.
    Loose debris embedded in a live sprite is not hard cover: shots can still hit the exposed unit.
    Actual downward debris movement entering a unit from outside causes 5 HP damage per cell,
    capped at 20 HP per unit per simulation tick. Readouts show debris speed and cumulative falling damage.

  
Troops and tanks target the nearest ground enemy. They advance until it is inside weapon range
    (troops 150 cells, tanks 210 cells between silhouettes), stop there to fire, and back off while
    still firing if the enemy closes within 70 / 100 cells, so opposing ground units no longer run into
    each other. If terrain, cover or shell range blocks the shot, they reposition toward the nearest
    enemy until they can hit it, even if that brings them closer and puts them at risk; only actual
    contact distance still makes them back off.
    Tanks drive in reverse (60% speed) without turning the hull and keep firing while backing up. The
    turret turns independently and can face opposite the treads. Shells fly a real gravity arc (185
    cells/s, 90 cells/s²): the barrel elevates through the tank's aim frames (up to about 32°)
    and the shell leaves the tip of the elevated barrel. This limits reach to about 340 cells on level
    ground, and targets too far, too high or too far below the barrel cannot be shot. While at least
    one troop rides inside, a troop gunner appears in the top hatch and fires bullets as well.
    Mechs (hoppers) are the toughest unit at 600 HP (tanks 400, planes 150, troops 100) and keep moving
    around a nearby enemy while firing, holding one flank then hopping over to the other side.
    A destroyed mech does not vanish: its body turns black and keeps falling under gravity while
    white light cones burst outward from its center in every direction, lengthening, widening and
    brightening for 1.5 s while each cone fades toward its tip; then it explodes. The blast radius is
    half the mech's size (half the mean of its collision width and height, about its own outline);
    it turns terrain and structures in that radius into debris and damages every unit inside it (up to
    220 HP, falling off with distance, friend or foe), so mechs can chain-detonate. During the fuse,
    troops and tanks inside the zone (plus an 8-cell margin) stop what they are doing and run straight
    away from the wreck, so survivors can get clear before it blows.
    Mechs only pick up tanks for an airlift when they are not fighting (no ground enemy within 260
    cells across / 160 cells vertically); a pickup still on approach is called off if an enemy appears,
    while a tank already gripped is carried through to the landing.

  
Hoppers use continuous aggressive assault: they advance toward the enemy base, rather than turning
    back toward distant enemies behind them. They jump over obstacles when clearance permits and
    fire whenever a clear path and reload permit. They do not bait, withhold shots to avoid damage,
    or retreat from hostile projectiles or falling debris. This also applies in generated-city combat.
    They accept lethal damage risk, but retain real collision, water/ceiling safety, jump charges
    and the clear-firing-path requirement. This is local strategy, not global pathfinding.

  
Automatic units check the swept weapon sprite path before firing: solid terrain, structures and debris
    block shots. Ground units advance to seek a clear angle; blocked hoppers try jumps, and jets climb.
    Other units retreat from unsupported debris overhead. Their retreat decisions persist for
    half a second to reduce direction jitter. Collision and water safety remain authoritative, so escape
    or a clear angle is not guaranteed. The selected-unit readout reports the current tactical decision.
    Manual fire remains an explicit override for testing material impacts.

  
Automated hoppers also sweep their actual collision-mask leading edge 24 cells ahead (farther
    at high speed), refreshing at 10 Hz or when commanded direction changes. Assault routing cancels
    blocked boost momentum but never reverses away from the enemy base; it attempts a safe forward
    jump or waits at actual collisions. Pickup approach is the deliberate exception to baseward movement.
    Takeoff checks 18 cells of overhead clearance before spending a jump charge.
    The carrying pose plays one whole hopper frame at a time (no stacked or spliced sprite parts): it reaches
    down with fall frames, clenches with jump frames 0–1, springs up with jump frames 2–3 and glides on
    fall frames, each shifted so its feet rest on the tank roof. Both units sweep collision masks one cell at a time.
    Loaded flight eases acceleration and braking at waypoints; the sequence only moves forward and
    settles on its last glide frame, avoiding repeated pose snaps. Shared quarter-cell preview offsets keep the payload attached,
    and obstacle climbs commit to a clearance segment rather than jittering between axes.
    Route decisions appear alongside tactical decisions. Manual movement retains its original controls.

  
Fit area shows the full battlefield; larger zoom levels scroll without changing physics or aim coordinates.
    For controlled unit tests, disable base spawning, reset, pause, then manually spawn the desired units.
    Manual spawning respects the same living-unit, lifetime and core limits as automatic spawning.

  
Both spawn paths check the actual initial collision mask against solid material, water and opposing
    units. Blocked attempts consume no lifetime slot and leave no registered unit behind. Automatic spawning
    retries after one second, without clearing debris or changing the queued unit type. Base status reports
    the most recent issuance or reason for waiting; friendly overlap retains the existing faction rule.

  
Select a firing unit from either faction: cyan brackets identify it, amber brackets identify the selected
    opposing target. Fire at target aims at a current occupied sprite pixel; it does not guarantee a hit or
    bypass terrain, buildings, reload time or projectile travel. The selected-unit readout shows HP, reload,
    position and current road-speed multiplier. Disable unit auto-fire for manual weapon tests.

  
Health bars and selection outlines follow the visible artwork, not transparent animation padding.
    Brief impact markers show hits: red for units, cyan for interceptions and amber for material.
    The recent-impact log reports actual HP lost and physical debris issued per hit; only the latest
    eight events are displayed from a bounded 32-event history. Misses leaving the area are labelled crop edge.

  
Each living base core may issue at most four simultaneous units and 64 lifetime units. A broken core
    stops subsequent spawning and its rooftop weapons; surviving troops remain active. Units use existing
    pixel collision, gravity, water drag/drowning and opposing-owner collision. Ground units wait at deep water, and troops check open drops so they stop dry at the bank;
    automated troops that cannot cross then roam the shore, wandering 14–56 cells inland, idling 0.7–2.7 s and strolling back to the edge, while still holding to fire at enemies in range;
    hopper jumps lunge forward, tanks accelerate/animate their tracks faster, and all four types move 1.8×
    comparison defaults.     No source GIF timing or original comparison controls are changed.

  
Bullets, shells and hopper missiles are swept projectiles with unit HP and real building/terrain impacts.
    Rooftop launchers steer missiles toward a fixed enemy-building point; interceptors steer toward actual
    hostile missiles and destroy both projectiles on swept proximity contact. These are real damaging/intercepting
    projectiles, not detached GIF streaks. They are bounded kinematic flight, not rigid-body rockets or blast pressure.
    Missiles stay compact throughout flight; the reference supplies particle styling, not an explosion
    animation. Impact or interception scatters 12 radial damaging fragments from the blast rim (12 HP, 160 cells/second,
    0.7-second lifetime). Fragments use the same swept collision and debris accounting and do not produce
    further fragments. Empty-air flight and lifetime expiry never trigger a burst. The shared 128-projectile
    cap also applies to fragments; diagnostics explicitly report any suppressed fragment issuance.
    Shells, debris and water share the existing physical particle world. Frame/core pixels have one building owner.
    Windows, wheat and amber storage fill are visual details, not additional physical material or production.

  
All fixture structures, units and ammo are explicitly free lab issuance. Barrier + debris + construction is
    audited against initial structure + converted terrain + issued ammo fragments; water and terrain are also
    audited. No HM/SM, saves, capture, city production or live-game files are integrated. Terrain raster updates
    at 30 Hz; physics and units remain 60 Hz, sprites are cached, diagnostics update at 4 Hz, and flight is capped
    at 128 projectiles with an eight-second lifetime. Measurements include rendering and active physics, report
    actual elapsed render/step rates and frame-time tails, and invalidate hidden-tab/error runs.

  
Faction-colored surface roads are generated along dry approaches between buildings and the terrain obstacles.
    Friendly grounded units move 25% faster only while fully supported by intact owned road cells.
    Enemies, jets, airborne units and wet units receive no road bonus. Damaged or flooded road support removes
    the bonus. Manual road placement and resource tunnels with miners and branching underground HM chambers
    remain future work.
