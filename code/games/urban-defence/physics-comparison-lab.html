Physics comparison lab

Three small, separate simulations use models already present in Urban Defence labs. They do not load or edit the live game world, saves, or resources. Use the controls to compare how they behave under the same fixed-step clock.

A · Continuous water

Fractional water volumes flow under gravity and level sideways. Open the divider and observe flow into the dry chamber.

Strength: stable, cheap flood-depth rules. Limitation: coarse pressure and no fluid force on structures.

B · Structural members

Connected beams, support loss, damage and collisions create falling debris. Blast the lower support to start a progressive collapse.

Strength: legible building-scale damage and repair. Limitation: a gameplay approximation, not engineering analysis.

C · Shared material grid

Terrain, barriers, water and debris occupy one cell each. Blast the left support and watch solid debris fall while water routes around it.

Strength: one authority for terrain, water and debris interactions. Limitation: grid-scale geometry; higher cost and larger-world performance need measurement.

D · Experimental hazards

Pour lava to watch it flow, conduct heat, damage nearby buildings and cool into basalt. Fire a limited-energy beam through a building or just below the ground surface; call a meteor for an impact crater.

Strength: heat conduction, energy transfer, cooling and phase changes create persistent local effects. Limitation: temperatures and material values are qualitative gameplay parameters, not real-world measurements.

Integration recommendation

Do not replace the live game's physics wholesale yet. The live game currently uses a heightfield plus separate building and water approximations. A staged approach is safer: keep heightfield and current economy/combat authoritative, selectively add tested cell-based interactions in small areas, and only expand after save compatibility and target-device benchmarks.

Arcade hazardsMemorable weapons and environmental eventsWorth prototyping as optional abilities after the core loop is stable; tune energy, cooling, crater size and counterplay before adding them to matches.

These are prototype comparisons, not evidence that a model is production-ready. The thermal model tracks per-cell enthalpy, conduction, ambient heat loss and simplified melt/freeze transitions; its temperatures, heat capacities and phase thresholds are qualitative gameplay parameters, not real-world material constants. Full-map simulation cost, mobile performance, deterministic saves, tunneling, pathing, and unit interaction remain integration gates.
