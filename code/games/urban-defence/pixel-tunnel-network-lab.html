Tunnel network lab

Each faction generates a surface entrance shaft, a trunk tunnel and a branch to its resource node. Crews survey the centre line with markers, dig while lining both sides with orange wall pixels, then clear the markers. When a tunnel reaches a node, two miners start hauling HM, dig out their own storage shaft and room, and fill it. The storage room then joins the pipeline highway: two separate 3 px tubes just under the surface, an SM tube (green casing, channel 2 px down, fed by farm silos) and an HM tube (gold casing, channel 5 px down) that drops HM into an underground cellar beneath each allied building. Cargo particles travel inside each dark channel. Blasted pipe cells flash red, then repair themselves one pixel at a time (flooded cells wait for the water to drain).

Dig tool: the surface line and every tunnel's outer wall glow; the section nearest the mouse glows cyan to show where the new tunnel will start. Click it, then move to preview the route and click again. The end snaps onto a nearby tunnel or onto the ground (with a pulsing ring), so hovering over the far side of the mountain digs a level pass all the way through. If the direct route is blocked the preview reroutes itself (water and structures are marked red and named in the tooltip by the mouse). Tunnels build straight through open caves, walling off the gap. Right-click or Esc cancels.

Surface roads: each faction gets a road from its entrance shaft past every building front. Building pads are terraced so the road stays level under each building and ramps between them. With Build road, the start and end snap to road ends (to join them), tunnel-pass floors or the ground. The preview grades the deck at 1:2 where it can (steeper ramps up to 1:1 are flagged), cuts bumps (orange), fills dips (tan), and bridges water and deep gaps with concrete decks on pillars (blue). Road ends snap from up to 12 px away (hovering anywhere on a road jumps to its nearest open end), so any road can be extended; a dashed guide links the mouse to the snapped surface marker. A road over a surface shaft lays a flush steel hatch (hazard-striped frame, manhole lid on the ladder): walkers cross it like the rest of the road and cannot fall in, the ladder no longer pokes up into the roadway, and the shaft below stays open for climbers. Roads refuse to overlap other roads, sit under water or cut the pipeline, and a cut deeper than 14 px asks for a tunnel pass instead; a road then follows the pass floor through the mountain. Roads pave one column at a time, repair blast damage on their own, and give allied ground units a ×1.25 speed bonus. Bridges take the faction's style (blue modern, red gothic) or the Bridge style choice; see the bridge sheet below.

Bridge styles

  
Each panel is a small river world with one road planned straight across it by the same road planner, in each
    building style. Only the deck (and pillars on dry land) are real wall pixels, so bridges never drain the river; the
    superstructure is drawn around it: Asian - a red covered "wind and rain" gallery with tiled roofs and pagodas
    on bracketed stone piers, or a red moon arch with end pavilions on short spans; Gothic - a stone viaduct of
    keystoned arches with a balustrade, spired towers and a pointed-arch arcade; Modern - a white tied arch with
    hanger cables over a slim girder on Y piers.
